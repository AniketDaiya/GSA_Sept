Role: You are a branding strategist who specializes in fast, high-concept pop-up brands built in a single sitting.

Context: I need a concept for a stall/booth at a college fest. It needs to be built around one real, relatable problem, and sell one specific product or service — not a broad category.

Task: Generate 3 distinct stall concepts. For each one, give:
1. A working name for the idea
2. The one specific problem it solves
3. One line on what makes it different from the obvious version of this idea

Constraints: Avoid generic, overused fest-stall concepts (chai stalls, generic photo booths, meme merch) unless meaningfully reinvented. Each concept must be realistic to build and pitch in one sitting — no complex logistics.

Output: A numbered list of exactly 3 concepts, each 2-3 lines, formatted cleanly and ready to scan quickly.

Replace the [placeholder]. This output is your entry text — keep it in your chat.

Role: You are a branding strategist and pitch writer who builds complete, investor-grade brand documents — the kind that reads like a real pitch deck in prose form, not a short summary.

Context: My stall idea is: [describe your idea here, OR write "concept #X from above" if you used Prompt 1].

Task: Write a complete brand pitch document with the following sections, in this order. Format each section title as a bold heading (no numbering, no list markers) exactly as written below:

**Brand Name & Tagline** — a sharp, memorable name and a one-line tagline that captures the whole concept.

**The Problem** — the real, specific problem people have, why existing options fail to solve it, and why it's frustrating enough that people would actually stop to hear about a fix.

**The Solution** — what this stall actually offers, how it works, and why it solves the problem better than what already exists.

**Who It's For** — split into Primary and Secondary audiences, each described specifically (not "everyone at the fest").

**What We Offer** — a short list of 2-4 specific products/services/experiences being sold or offered.

**How It Works** — how someone actually interacts with the stall, step by step, on fest day.

**Business Model** — how it makes sense as a real offering (pricing structure, membership, per-item, or however this concept would realistically sustain itself).

**Why Us** — the specific reason this beats a generic version of the same idea, a competitor, or an AI/template shortcut. Be pointed and confident here.

**Where We're At** — frame this as pre-launch/pilot stage, tied to this fest as the first real test.

**The Ask** — one line framed around why the room/judges at the fest should back this stall over every other one there (not an investor ask — this is a live pitch-night ask).

**Stall Aesthetics & Physical Setup** — the vibe (compare it to something recognizable), color palette with reasoning behind each color choice, one focal decor/centerpiece element, and signage details (what each banner/sign says).

**Fest Day Activations** — one or two interactive, low-barrier hooks that would make a passerby actually stop (a lead magnet, a game, a challenge, a giveaway mechanic — something people can do in under a minute).

**60-Second Pitch Script** — an actual spoken script structured as: open with the problem → name the brand → describe the stall visual → close with the single reason it beats every other stall at the fest.

Constraints: Every section must be specific and concrete — no vague language, no filler like "engaging experience" or "everyone will love it." Ground every claim in the actual problem and audience named in The Problem and Who It's For. The Stall Aesthetics & Physical Setup section must be vivid and photographic enough to hand directly to an image generator afterward. Before finalizing, identify the single weakest section yourself and strengthen it in the final version rather than flagging it.

Output: The sections above, each under its own bold heading with no numbers or bullet-style markers on the headings themselves, written in full prose (like a real pitch document), clean and formatted exactly as it should appear when pasted directly into a Google Doc.
